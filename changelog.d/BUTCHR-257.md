### Fixed
- **`test/unit/brief-tool-surface.test.ts` (BUTCHR-48) now enforces the REVERSE coverage direction it previously only implied (BUTCHR-257).** It always asserted "every backticked verb a brief instructs an agent to call exists in the real tool registry" — a verb that ships in `atlassianTools()` but is named by NO brief passed silently, and nothing in the test, its name, or its doc comment said so. Measured at `99bfdf2` (`atlassianTools()` keys intersected against the guard's own `extractVerbs` rule applied to `briefs/*.md`, not a hand-written list on either side): **registry size 32, taught by at least one brief 24, taught by NO brief 8** — `confluence_create_page`, `confluence_get_page`, `confluence_update_page`, `jira_assign`, `jira_create_issue`, `jira_link_issues`, `jira_set_priority`, `jira_transition`. 25% of the surface — a systematic pattern, not one or two defensible absentees.
- New `src/tools/brief-coverage.ts` exports `BRIEF_COVERAGE`, a per-verb declaration keyed by every registry verb: `{ taught: true }` or `{ taught: false, reason: "..." }` (a discriminated union — a verb can never be both or neither, by construction, unlike a pair of hand-written positive/exclusion lists). Kept adjacent to the registry, the same way `alias-audit.ts` already sits beside `defs.ts` for the alias-classification half of this same registry.
- `brief-tool-surface.test.ts` gained a new describe block asserting: (1) every `atlassianTools()` verb has exactly one `BRIEF_COVERAGE` entry, and no entry names a verb no longer in the registry (a genuinely new verb, or a stale entry, both fail loud — this is what makes "adding a verb to the registry FAILS THE SUITE until someone declares whether a brief must teach it" executable); (2) every `taught: false` entry carries a non-empty reason; (3) every `taught: true` entry is corroborated by a REAL `briefs/*.md` file naming that verb via the same `extractVerbs` rule the forward check uses — a claimed-but-unwritten teaching turns the suite red, not just the declaration false; (4) a non-vacuity test proving the three accounting functions distinguish good from bad on synthetic fixtures, and — fed the REAL `BRIEF_COVERAGE` a sentinel verb it has never heard of — still catch it, same sentinel technique as `merge-check-guard.test.ts`'s `findUnaccountedBriefTypes` tests.
- Deliberately does NOT declare per-tier expected coverage (which of task/story/epic/project should teach a verb) — several `taught: true` verbs are legitimately taught by only a subset of tiers, and an "expected tier set" per verb would be a second hand-written list to keep in sync with the first. Also deliberately does NOT derive `taught: false` reasons from `aliasTag`/`AliasClass` — the 8 untaught verbs happen to be exactly the aliasTag-carrying deprecated aliases today, which is evidence the exclusions are principled, not a coupling to build the mechanism on; reasons are written independently from each verb's own description in `defs.ts`.
- The existing forward check is unchanged and unweakened: `extractVerbs` was not touched, and a fake verb named in a brief still fails it exactly as before (re-verified adversarially as part of this change).
- Mutation-verified at this ticket's own tree: (a) removing a `taught: true` entry for a real verb turns the new "every verb has exactly one entry" test red, naming the verb; (b) adding an entry for a verb not in the registry turns the "no stale entry" half red, naming it; (c) flipping a real `taught: false` verb to `taught: true` turns the "corroborated by a real brief" test red, naming it; (d) injecting a genuinely new verb into `atlassianTools()` turns the "every verb has exactly one entry" test red, naming it — DoD #1's own literal criterion, tested the way it's worded; (e) reverting each mutation returns to a clean `git status` and a green suite.
- **Review round 1 finding, closed:** `findFalselyTaughtVerbs` only ever corroborated `taught: true` entries — a `taught: false` entry's "no brief teaches this" reason was never re-checked in either direction, so a reason could silently go stale the moment a brief started naming that verb. Measured: appending a one-line mention of `jira_transition` (declared `taught: false`) to `briefs/task.md` left the suite green. New `findTaughtFalseButNamedVerbs` closes the converse: a `taught: false` entry a brief now names fails loud, naming the verb and its stale reason. Its known, explicitly-documented limit (inherited from `extractVerbs`, not new): a bare mention that WARNS against a verb is indistinguishable from one that TEACHES it, so a future "never call `X`" sentence can trip this — a deliberate trade-off (occasional human judgment call over permanent silent staleness), argued in `brief-coverage.ts`'s header the same way `merge-check-guard.test.ts`'s `BASE_MERGE_CAVEAT` argues its own known hole.
