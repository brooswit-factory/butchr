### Added
- **`/health` now reports per-detector "could not check" coverage, so a reader can tell "checked, nothing found" apart from "could not check" without grepping the daemon log (BUTCHR-179).** Several detectors (`stalled.ts`, `parked.ts`, `frozen-asleep.ts`, `crash-loop.ts`, `reconcile-failure.ts`, `escalation-loop.ts`) were already correctly treating a failed Atlassian comments/links fetch as a THIRD outcome, never collapsed into "confirmed clean" — but every one of them expressed that outcome as, at most, a `WARNING` log line plus the absence of a write. A log line is not a surface a reader consults, so nothing visible distinguished a ticket that was checked and is fine from one that was never checked this poll.
- New `src/daemon/coverage.ts`: a name-agnostic `CoverageTracker` (`recordChecked(name)` / `recordDeclined(name)` / `snapshot()`) — in-memory counters plus a last-decline timestamp per detector name, exactly like the existing `LoopHealth`/`StalledTracker` liveness trackers. General by construction: it never enumerates or imports the detectors that call it.
- `combineHealth` (`src/daemon/health.ts`) gains a third, optional `coverage` parameter, surfaced as a `coverage` sibling field on `HealthStatus` — alongside `build` (BUTCHR-54), never inside `components[]`, and never able to flip `ok`: a detector declining to verify one ticket during a transient Atlassian degradation does not mean the daemon itself is unhealthy, and conflating the two would make an uptime checker treat "restart the daemon" as the fix for an external outage a restart cannot touch.
- Wired into two modules as a proven, tested consumer pair (deliberately not all declining call sites — see the ticket's own report for the full measured set and why the increment stayed narrow): `src/labels/sync.ts`'s `stalled.check()` branch (the pre-existing three-state consumer this ticket promoted to the standard every new consumer must match) and `src/agents/escalation-loop.ts`'s `escalateUnresponsive` (the sustained-unresponsive alarm). Each records `checked` for a resolved fetch (found or genuinely confirmed not-found) and `declined` for a rejected one — proven with tests that a naive `x === true`/`!x` collapse would fail, and mutation-tested (the recording removed, the exact new tests fail by name, restored).
